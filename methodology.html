How Namesprig counts baby names

Where Namesprig’s baby name numbers come from (Social Security card applications), what they leave out, and exactly how ranks, shares, trends and state rates are worked out.

Data: Social Security card applications, 1880 to 2025

SSA files dated Apr 13, 2026

Sourced

Sources

  1. US Social Security Administration, national baby names data (names.zip): babies by first name, sex and year, 1880 to 2025. Public domain (US government work).Files dated Apr 13, 2026; downloaded Oct 22, 2026.
  2. US Social Security Administration, state-specific data (namesbystate.zip): the same by state and DC, 1910 to 2025. Public domain.Files dated Apr 10, 2026; downloaded Oct 22, 2026.

Namesprig isn't affiliated with Social Security. SSA publishes the files once a year, in spring; we download them by hand and rebuild every page.

What the counts include

The counts are babies born in the US whose parents applied for a Social Security card for them, by the first name and sex on the application. Almost every US-born child gets one, but not quite all, so the counts are a little under the true number of births.

To protect privacy, SSA leaves out any name given to fewer than 5 babies of a sex in a year (in a state, in the state files). So very rare names and spellings aren't here, and totals for uncommon names are floors. Different spellings (Aiden and Aidan) are different names, exactly as SSA lists them.

Namesprig has pages for the 6,092 names most given since 1880 plus every name in this year's top 1,000 of either sex. There are no meanings or origins: we have no sourced data for them, so we don't show any.

What each label means

Sourced
A count copied straight from the SSA files: babies given a name in a year, a state's babies, the number of different names.
Computed
Arithmetic on those counts, by the rules below. Anyone with the same files gets the same figure.
Estimated
Nothing on Namesprig is estimated, modelled or generated.

How the computed figures work

  • Rank: a name's place among girls' or boys' names by babies that year, most first, as SSA orders them. Girls and boys are ranked separately.
  • Share per 10,000 (Compare): babies given the name per 10,000 babies of both sexes recorded that year, so years with more births don't look more popular.
  • 10-year trend: the name's rank in 2025 against 2015, in the sex it is mostly given to. "New" means outside the top 1,000 in 2015.
  • Peak year: the year the most babies of either sex got the name.
  • Girls' share: girls over all babies with the name, since 1880 or over the last 10 years. "Unisex" means each sex had at least a third over the last 10 years.
  • State rate per 1,000: a state's babies with the name per 1,000 babies of that sex listed in the state that year.
  • "vs US rate" (state pages): the name's share of the state's babies of that sex over its share nationally. Only names with 25 or more babies in the state, so a handful of births can't make a name look distinctive.
  • Climbers (year pages): names in that year's top 500 that rose the most places from the year before.

How we check it

Every build runs known-answer tests: totals and the #1 names of 2025 are checked against the raw SSA file, 1880's number ones (Mary and John) against SSA's published table, and the explorer and compare arithmetic against hand-worked examples. Spot a mistake? Use Contact and corrections.
