What makes a middle name work

Say the whole name out loud. A middle name usually sounds best with a different beat from its neighbours, so a one-beat name between two longer ones (Eleanor Jane Whitaker) or a long one between two short ones (Jack Sebastian Cole). It also helps when words don't blur: a name ending in the sound the next one starts with runs together, and two vowels meeting make a stumble.

How the names are ranked

Each of this year's top names gets points for a beat count different from both neighbours and for a clean join at each gap. It loses points where one name runs into the next, where two vowels meet, where it shares a first letter with a neighbour, and where it rhymes with the first name. Initials that spell a rude or unlucky word (like P.I.G.) are left out. Ties go to the more popular name. Syllables are counted by vowel groups, so the odd name will be a beat off. This is a rule of thumb, not a measure of taste; it runs in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere.
